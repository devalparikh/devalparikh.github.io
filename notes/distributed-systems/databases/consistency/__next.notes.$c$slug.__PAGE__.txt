<h2 id="types-levels-of-consistency">Types (levels of consistency)</h2>
<h3 id="strong-consistency-read-after-write">Strong consistency (read after write)</h3>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Updates can be read instantly after a write (higher latency)</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>using <a href="/notes/distributed-systems/databases/consensus/">Consensus</a> (internally to the distributed storage system) to implement</p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="eventual-consistency">Eventual consistency</h3>
<ul>
<li>Updates can be read after x time after updates (lower latency, but risk of stale data)</li>
<li>A read may not read at a valid state as it is eventually consistent but maybe not at the time of read</li>
<li>Consistency refers to reading a valid state at any time all the time</li>
</ul>
<p>When there are multiple storage systems in a system, synchronization has to be done somehow to align them</p>
<p>note:</p>
<ul>
<li>Consistency in CAP is a specific type of strong consistency in the scenario of network partitions</li>
</ul>
